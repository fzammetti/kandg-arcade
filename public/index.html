A humor time capsule

Some of the humor in this game has NOT aged well, and this port is pretty close to 100% faithful. So, you've been warned.

In 2004, my friend Anthony Volpe and I were thinking about following up on the rousing success (ahem, sorry, that's a weird way to spell "abject failure") of our PocketPC game Invasion: Trivia!. We had two characters we liked in Krelmac and Gentoo, so the natural question was what game can we make with them?

I remembered a game from the Commodore 64 days that I always loved called Lazy Jones. It was frankly kind of a fever dream where you run around a 2D funhouse, dodging waiters and their carts and other things, looking for doors. Behind the doors were all sorts of weird little mini-games. I remember one, for example, where you played the part of a fork... yeah, the thing you eat with... and had to launch yourself at a turkey. The obstacle to your goal? A telephone that flew around.

Like I said: fever dream!

But, it was also exactly the kind of thing Anthony and I would have come up with. So, we decided to...ahem... take inspiration from... Lazy Jones. We'd make a game where Krelmac and Gentoo abduct you, and you'd have to go through their ship playing mini-games in the hopes of escaping. Not quite as far out there as Lazy Jones I guess, but good enough.

So, I got to work on the coding, while Anthony worked up the graphics. I think we banged the entire game out in about two months start to finish, including cut scenes and voice work, and 25 mini-games. These games took... again, AHEM... "inspiration"... from lots of games we love. Frogger became Cosmic Squirrel. Kaboom! became Far Out Fowl. Space Invaders became A Day In The Strife. And so on.

On April 6, 2004, K&G Arcade released to PocketPC owners worldwide. I forget the asking price frankly, but I think it was something like $4.99 or so. Well, we wound up making MAYBE $250 total from it over the course of a few months.

So, you know, just like Invasion: Trivia!, we had another total success on our hands.

As with Invasion: Trivia!, we had a lot of fun making this game, so it doesn't feel like it was a failure at all. I still have very fond memories of recording the voiceovers with Anthony, of going back-and-forth on the scripts... oh, and it is at this point that I really need to mention that some of the humor in this game has NOT aged well. But, as with Invasion: Trivia!, I made this port pretty close to 100% faithful. In fact, the only changes I made were some outright bugs that would have caused crashes even on the original (apologies to anyone who DID buy it almost 25 years ago!). So, you've been warned.

A final technical note: this port was 100% vibe-coded with Claude. As opposed to Invasion: Trivia!, where I started out hand-porting it, and got about 50% of the way before AI got good enough to help me finish, K&G Arcade was start to finish done with AI and I honestly haven't even looked at the code. That's not generally how I work with AI, but given what this is it doesn't bother me one bit. It ALSO doesn't bother me one bit that it took a grand total of about 8 hours start to finish. Yes, Claude was able to understand a nearly 25-year old C++ codebase that used a defunct library (GAPIDraw), as well as a custom cutscene engine, and was able to get this working pretty flawlessly with very little prompting from me, even pointing out things I had botched back then. The only thing of note I gave it was the Invasion: Trivia! port, telling it to use it as an example and guide for how to do this and generally structure things. Seriously impressive what it was able to pull off if you ask me. What we're getting from AI nowadays is pretty astounding. And yes, I share all the same worries about it in terms of what it means for coders, what it means for the workforce, what it means for the environment, what it means in legal terms, and so on and so on. There's A LOT to be worried about. But, at the same time, as someone who has been a technologist for over 50 years now, I can't help but kinda love some of the goodness we also undoubtedly can get from it. And a port of a nearly 25-year old game of mine is definitely on the list.
